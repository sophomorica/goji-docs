## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can show that 1/2, 2/4, and 4/8 are the same amount wearing different names — and generate an equivalent for any simple fraction.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 3 | The Eat Me cake: "Alice cuts her cake in 2 and eats 1 piece. The White Rabbit cuts an identical cake in 4 and eats 2. Who ate more cake?" Let the argument happen before resolving | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 3 | Watch: the same cake re-cut — 1/2 becomes 2/4 becomes 4/8 with the *amount visibly unchanged*; the trap (2/3 vs 2/4) shows matching numerators are not matching amounts | animation (parent device preview for now) | `anim.math.35.fractions.01` |
| 3 | **Guided practice** | 5 | Fold one paper strip in half, shade 1/2; fold again — count 2/4; again — 4/8. Same shading the whole time. Then child generates: "Show me another name for 1/3" (fold thirds, then sixths) | paper strips, journal | — |
| 4 | **Practice** | 8 | Fractions module equivalence session, ≥ 85% | math → fractions | — |
| 5 | **Comprehension** | 5 | Quiz incl. naming two equivalents for a given fraction; journal: "2/4 is the same as 1/2 because ___" | parent quiz, journal | `quiz.math.35.fractions.02` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Math — fractions module equivalence session, accuracy ≥ 85%
- [ ] Task 2: Parent quiz — `quiz.math.35.fractions.02`, pass ≥ 8/10
- [ ] Task 3: Journal — "Write one 'same amount, different cuts' sentence: name two equivalent fractions and explain why they show the same amount."
  - Checklist: Two equivalent fractions (like 1/2 and 2/4) · The words 'same amount' · Why more cuts don't change the amount

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** during folding, ask "did the amount change?" at each fold — hesitation means the idea hasn't landed; keep folding before moving to the screen.
- **Summative:** module ≥ 85% and quiz ≥ 8/10 including one generate-your-own equivalent.
- **If not yet:** more physical re-cutting (paper, a sliced sandwich) before symbols; then re-watch the animation. If the child multiplies top and bottom by different numbers, name the move out loud: "whatever the cuts do to the pieces, they do to the shading" — and show it with one more fold.
