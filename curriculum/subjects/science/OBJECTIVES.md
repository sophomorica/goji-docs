**Misconceptions to watch (strand-level):** "only pushes cause motion — pulls don't" (pulling a wagon handle or rope moves it too); "a stronger push doesn't matter" (gentle vs strong travel visibly different distances; a stuck wagon needs the stronger push); "things move by themselves" (untouched swing/wagon never starts — deliberate contrast with `obj.science.k2.living.01`, where living things DO move on their own); "a push and a pull are the same direction" (push = away, pull = toward).

**Additional misconceptions to watch (strand-level, at-a-distance forces):** "magnets attract ALL metal / everything" (the aluminum can and the wax crayon never move — only iron/steel); "heavy things fall, light things float" (the feather falls too — slowly, because air pushes up on it); "you must touch something to move it" (gravity and magnets act across a gap, even through paper); "any two magnets snap together" (same ends N–N push apart; only different ends snap).

**Misconceptions to watch (strand-level, motion):** "spinning and moving in a circle are the same" (the top turns in ONE spot; the merry-go-round horse travels around a path — the classic 1.2 confusion); "sound just happens — nothing moves" (every sound comes from something vibrating: drum skin, guitar string, rubber band; you can see and feel the movement); "if I can't see it move, it isn't moving" (a humming string looks blurry-still — slow it down and it's fast back-and-forth); motion-word swaps (calling back-and-forth "straight there and straight back" — the repeat is what names it).

**Misconceptions to watch (strand-level, materials):** "cutting changes what it's made of" (small pieces of paper are STILL paper; half a wax candle is still wax — size changed, material didn't); "all thin things are see-through" (a thin ruler and thin cardboard block all light; thickness and transparency are different properties — echoes the K.3 big-vs-heavy split); "object and material are the same word" (the spoon is the object; wood or metal is what it's made of — same object, different materials); "some light through = broken/dirty glass" (wax paper letting some light through is a property of the material, not a flaw).

**Misconceptions to watch (strand-level):** "big = heavy" (a big balloon is lighter than a little rock — size and weight are different properties); mixing sort rules mid-sort (kept sorting by color after the rule changed to size — the rule, not the object, decides the group); property-word confusion (answering a shape word when asked for color, a size word when asked how it feels).

**Additional misconceptions to watch (strand-level, phases):** "sand/powder is a liquid because it pours" (each grain is a tiny solid that keeps its shape); "gas = nothing / air is nothing" (a gas is real matter that fills all the space — the balloon proves it); "melting makes a new different stuff" (melted chocolate is still chocolate; frozen juice is still juice); "only water freezes/melts" (the same phase changes happen to chocolate, juice, and more).

**Misconceptions to watch (strand-level, water):** "ice is not water" (it's frozen water — melt it and the puddle is the same water); "steam is air" (or smoke — it's water as a gas, floating up); "water can flow uphill" (it never climbs by itself — it always finds the low path); form confusion (which of ice/liquid/steam a picture shows).
