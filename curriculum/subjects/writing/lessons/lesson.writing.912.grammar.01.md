## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the learner treats sentence length, structure, and the colon/semicolon/dash as deliberate style choices — and can rewrite the same paragraph two ways and defend the difference.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 4 | Read two versions of the same paragraph aloud: one all mid-length compound sentences, one with varied lengths and a short punch sentence. Same facts, different pulse. Which writer do you trust more, and why? | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 8 | The three marks as *moves*, not rules: **semicolon** — a hinge between two sentences whose ideas lean on each other; **colon** — a drumroll (what follows delivers what was promised); **dash** — an interruption or spotlight, the most informal of the three. Then the rhythm principle: a short sentence after three long ones hits like a hammer *because* of the contrast. One model passage dissected from the current book — Dickens and London are on-shelf sources of all three marks | reader, (off-device) | — |
| 3 | **Practice** | 12 | Rewrite lab in the writing app: take one paragraph (own recent work, or a passage from `call-of-wild` / `little-women`) and produce two deliberate versions — (a) maximum flow: longer sentences, semicolons doing the joining; (b) maximum punch: varied lengths, one colon, one intentional short sentence. Both must stay grammatical | writing | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 8 | Write the rationale: 3–5 sentences in notebooks on which version fits which purpose (a letter? an argument? a story's climax?). Parent reviews against the rubric below | notebooks, writing | rubric with lesson (below) |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Writing — the two-version rewrite (both versions saved)
- [ ] Task 2: Notebooks — rationale paragraph
- [ ] Task 3: Parent review — score against the rubric below; discuss, don't just grade

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** during the rewrite lab, ask "why that mark, there?" — a shrug means the mark is decoration, not a decision; send them back to the model passage.
- **Summative:** parent rubric review of rewrite + rationale (per `obj.writing.912.grammar.01` — this objective is rubric-checked, not quiz-scored).
- **If not yet:** imitation before invention — copy one Stevenson or London paragraph by hand, then write a new paragraph on a different topic *stealing its exact sentence-length pattern and punctuation*. Structure borrowed consciously becomes structure owned.

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** writing app, notebooks, reader, parent review (manual sign-off per PARENT_STANDING.md conventions).
- **Blocked on new tool:** nothing.

## 6. Authoring notes

- Per [`../OBJECTIVES.md`](../OBJECTIVES.md), the check is a **rubric with the lesson**, not a quiz. Rubric (v1) — parent scores each 0/1/2, pass ≥ 6/8:
  | Criterion | 0 | 1 | 2 |
  |-----------|---|---|---|
  | Both versions grammatical | errors throughout | scattered errors | clean |
  | Versions actually differ in structure (not just word swaps) | same skeleton | some restructuring | genuinely different rhythm |
  | Marks used correctly (semicolon joins full sentences; colon delivers; dash interrupts) | misused | mostly right | correct and purposeful |
  | Rationale names effects, not rules ("the short sentence lands the point" beats "I used a semicolon") | restates rules | some effect-talk | argues from effect |
- Book sampling caveat: the 9–12 shelf is **empty on device** ([`../../reading/reading-lists.md`](../../reading/reading-lists.md) audit) — until Austen/Dickens are ingested, use the strongest 6–8 titles (`call-of-wild`, `little-women`, `treasure-island`) as model prose; they carry all three marks.
- This lesson pairs naturally with any essay assignment from the (future) compose strand — variety practiced on prose the learner cares about sticks best.
