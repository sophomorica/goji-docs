## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can label the job each word is doing in a real sentence — noun, verb, adjective, adverb, pronoun, conjunction — and knows that the same word can take different jobs in different sentences.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 3 | Parent writes "play" and asks: noun or verb? Trick question — "We play outside" vs "The play starts at noon." Words don't *have* jobs; they *take* jobs, sentence by sentence | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 4 | Watch: the sentence-machine — six job stations, words reporting for work, one word switching jobs | animation (parent device preview for now) | `anim.writing.35.grammar.01` |
| 3 | **Practice** | 10 | Label hunt in a real book: open the child's current read (e.g. `wizard-of-oz` or `black-beauty`), pick 3–4 sentences, and label every word's job together — parent takes the first, child takes the rest. Then flashcards: parts-of-speech deck, one pass | reader, flashcards | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 6 | Quiz: label the underlined word's job in book-flavored sentences (includes one linking-verb item — "was" is still a verb) | parent quiz | `quiz.writing.35.grammar.01` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Reading — 15 min in current book; label every word's job in 2 sentences (say it to the parent)
- [ ] Task 2: Flashcards — parts-of-speech deck, one pass
- [ ] Task 3: Parent quiz — `quiz.writing.35.grammar.01`, pass ≥ 8/10 (success criteria wants ≥ 8/10 across **2 sessions** — assign again later in the week)

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** in the label hunt, the tells are (a) adjectives vs adverbs ("describing word" is not one job — which thing does it describe?) and (b) linking verbs ("was" doesn't look like an action).
- **Summative:** quiz ≥ 8/10 on two different days (per `obj.writing.35.grammar.01`).
- **If not yet:** shrink to two jobs at a time — nouns vs verbs until solid, then add adjectives/adverbs as a pair (they're confusable, teach them against each other), then pronouns/conjunctions. Six at once overloads; two at a time sticks.
