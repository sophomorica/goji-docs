## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can tell whether a group of words is a complete sentence — it has a naming part (who) and a telling part (what happened) — and can fix a fragment by adding the missing part.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 2 | Parent says a fragment out loud and just… stops: "The big dog." Wait. The child's "…and WHAT?" itch IS the lesson — a fragment feels unfinished before anyone names the rule | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 3 | Watch: naming part + telling part snap together; fragments wobble and can't stand alone | animation (parent device preview for now) | `anim.writing.k2.grammar.01` |
| 3 | **Practice** | 8 | Sentence-or-not game, out loud: parent alternates real sentences and fragments (both kinds — missing who, missing what-happened); child calls "sentence!" or "broken!" and fixes the broken ones by adding the missing part. Pull a couple of lines from Aesop (`aesop-fables`): "The tortoise kept walking." vs "The speedy hare." | (off-device, book from reader) | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 5 | Quiz: pick the complete sentence / name the missing part; then journal: write 3 complete sentences about anything | parent quiz, journal | `quiz.writing.k2.grammar.01` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Parent quiz — `quiz.writing.k2.grammar.01`, pass ≥ 8/10
- [ ] Task 2: Journal — "Write 3 complete sentences of your own. Each one needs a naming part (who) and a telling part (what happened)."
  - Checklist: Sentence 1 has a who and a what-happened · Sentence 2 has a who and a what-happened · Sentence 3 has a who and a what-happened · Read each aloud — does it sound finished?
- [ ] Task 3: Reading — 10 min in Aesop; find one sentence and say its two parts

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** during the sentence-or-not game, does the child catch BOTH fragment kinds? Missing telling part ("The big dog.") is easy; missing naming part ("Ran down the hill.") is the tell.
- **Summative:** quiz ≥ 8/10 + 3 complete journal sentences.
- **If not yet:** replay `anim.writing.k2.grammar.01`, then go physical — write naming parts and telling parts on separate cards and let the child snap pairs together (silly combos welcome: "The tortoise ate my shoe." still counts — it has both parts).

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** parent quiz, journal, reader (Aesop for sample sentences).
- **Blocked on new tool:** on-device animation player (TOOLS.md §6A) — until then, parent-device preview. No dedicated grammar drill module exists; the parent-quiz item bank carries the practice load for this strand.

## 6. Authoring notes

- Animation brief: [`anim.writing.k2.grammar.01`](../../../assets/animations/anim.writing.k2.grammar.01.md).
- Quiz blueprint: `quiz.writing.k2.grammar.01` in [`../quiz-blueprints.md`](../quiz-blueprints.md).
- Vocabulary discipline: say **naming part** and **telling part** at this band, not "subject/predicate" — the grown-up words arrive in 3–5.
- Misconception to watch: "long = sentence, short = not." Keep "Birds sing." in the game — two words, complete.
