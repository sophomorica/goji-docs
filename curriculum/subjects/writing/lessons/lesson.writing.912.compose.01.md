## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the student can write an analytical essay on a novel: an arguable thesis, evidence with quoted lines woven into their own sentences, a counterclaim addressed honestly, and paragraphs that each advance the argument.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 5 | Parent offers two openings on the anchor novel: "This book is about a dog named Buck" vs "Buck's transformation argues that civilization is a coat, not a skin." Which one could someone *disagree* with? That one is a thesis; the other is a book report | (off-device, anchor novel) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 12 | **Parent models live** (animation: none — modeled live): build the essay's argument map in notebooks — thesis at top, each body paragraph as a sub-claim, each sub-claim owing the map a quoted line. Model **weaving** a quote (own sentence carries it) vs **dropping** one (quote sits alone, proving nothing). Then the counterclaim: the strongest honest reading *against* the thesis, and why the thesis survives it | notebooks | none — modeled live |
| 3 | **Practice** | 20 | Student builds their own argument map for a prompt-bank thesis question: thesis, 2–3 sub-claims, a quoted line hunted from the reader for each, counterclaim noted. Parent reviews the **map** before any drafting — bad maps make bad essays cheaply; bad drafts make them expensively | notebooks, reader | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 40 | Draft the essay from the approved map (session 2–3), ≥ 500 words. Parent reviews against the rubric below | writing | — |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Reading — anchor novel close-read passages + minutes
- [ ] Task 2: Notebooks — argument map (thesis, sub-claims, quotes, counterclaim); parent approves before drafting
- [ ] Task 3: Writing (via journal) — "Draft your essay from the argument map your parent approved: thesis, sub-claims, woven quotes, counterclaim." (starter ideas: [`../prompt-banks/912.md`](../prompt-banks/912.md))
  - Checklist: A thesis that is arguable and specific to THIS book · Every body paragraph advances the thesis (delete test) · Quotes woven in, each proving its sub-claim · The strongest honest counterclaim, addressed · At least 500 words
- [ ] Task 4: Parent review — rubric sign-off (parent-judged, never auto-scored)

## 4. Checks

- **Parent rubric (the gate):** □ thesis is arguable and specific to *this* book · □ every body ¶ advances the thesis (delete test: would the argument miss it?) · □ quotes woven, not dropped, and each actually proves its sub-claim · □ counterclaim is the strongest honest one, addressed not dodged · □ ≥ 500 words.
- **Formative:** the map review is the formative check — a thesis that's really a topic ("this essay is about courage") gets caught there, before 500 words are spent on it.
- **Summative (per `obj.writing.912.compose.01`):** rubric passes on 2 essays across the year.
- **If not yet:** thesis-is-a-topic → drill: turn 5 topics into claims ("courage" → "the novel treats courage as contagious"). Quotes dropped → rewrite one body ¶ weaving its quote three different ways. Counterclaim dodged → run the steelman drill from `lesson.writing.68.compose.02` at essay depth.
