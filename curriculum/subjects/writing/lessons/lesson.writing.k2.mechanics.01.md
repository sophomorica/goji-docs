## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child starts every sentence with a capital, ends it with the right mark (. ? !), and capitalizes names and I — in the quiz and, more importantly, in their own journal.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 2 | Parent writes (paper or journal) "my dog ate my sock" — no capital, no end mark. "Something's missing. Can you feel it?" A sentence with no capital and no period is like leaving the house half-dressed | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 4 | Three rules, three voices: capital = "a sentence stands up tall at the start"; the end mark matches the voice — telling voice (.), asking voice (?), big-feeling voice (!). Parent reads one sentence three ways; child picks the mark for each. Then: names and I are special — they wear capitals everywhere | (off-device) | — |
| 3 | **Practice** | 8 | Fix-it round: parent writes 5–6 broken sentences (missing capitals, wrong/missing end marks, lowercase names) and the child repairs them out loud or on paper. Include one from Aesop (`aesop-fables`) stripped bare: "the lion let the mouse go" → child restores it | (off-device, book from reader) | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 5 | Quiz: pick the correctly written sentence / choose the end mark; then journal: 2 sentences, child self-checks capitals and end marks before showing the parent | parent quiz, journal | `quiz.writing.k2.mechanics.01` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Parent quiz — `quiz.writing.k2.mechanics.01`, pass ≥ 8/10
- [ ] Task 2: Journal — "Write 2 sentences about your day. Self-check before parent sign-off: capitals where they belong, and an end mark on each."
  - Checklist: 2 complete sentences · A capital at each sentence start — and on names · An end mark (. ! ?) on each sentence · Self-check done before showing the parent
- [ ] Task 3: Reading — point at 3 end marks in today's book and name the voice each one makes

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** in the fix-it round, does the child catch missing capitals on **names**, not just at sentence starts? Names are the usual miss.
- **Summative:** quiz ≥ 8/10 — and the real one: journal entries this week show the habit without prompting.
- **If not yet:** make the child the teacher — parent writes deliberately broken sentences daily and the child grades them with a red crayon. Kids catch errors as inspectors long before they stop making them as writers; the inspector's eye migrates.

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** parent quiz, journal, reader.
- **Blocked on new tool:** nothing. (A future journal lint — gentle underline for missing capital/end mark — would reinforce this daily; candidate for TOOLS.md when the writing vertical is prioritized.)

## 6. Authoring notes

- Prereq: `lesson.writing.k2.grammar.01` (you punctuate *sentences* — the child must know what one is first).
- Quiz blueprint: `quiz.writing.k2.mechanics.01` in [`../quiz-blueprints.md`](../quiz-blueprints.md).
- Misconception to watch: over-capitalization — once capitals feel important, some kids Capitalize Every Big Word. The quiz carries a distractor for exactly this.
- This objective is a **habit**, not a fact: expect the quiz to pass weeks before the journal is clean. Keep the journal check running.
