## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can argue a claim in writing with real evidence — and state the other side fairly before answering it, because persuasion that hides the other side is just noise.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 4 | Socratic starter: "Was Long John Silver a villain?" Whatever the child says, parent argues the other side — using the book. When the child gets frustrated: "Notice that you can't beat me by repeating yourself louder. You have to answer my *reasons*." | (off-device, `treasure-island`) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 8 | **Parent models live** (animation: none — modeled live): draft the argument's skeleton aloud — claim (arguable, not a fact), 2 evidence points from the book, then the move that separates persuasion from propaganda: "Someone could fairly say ___" stated **so well the other side would nod**, then answered. Name the crime: the strawman (a fake-weak version of the other side) | (off-device) | none — modeled live |
| 3 | **Practice** | 10 | Steelman drill: child takes their claim from the hook, writes the *other side's* best point first (parent judges: "would I nod?"), then their answer to it. Only then the evidence paragraphs | writing, reader | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 15 | Solo persuasive piece from the prompt bank, ≥ 250 words: claim, ≥ 2 evidence points, counterargument stated fairly + answered. Parent reviews against checklist | writing | — |

## 4. Checks

- **Parent checklist (the gate):** □ claim is arguable (a reasonable person could disagree) · □ ≥ 2 evidence points that support it · □ counterargument present, stated fairly (nod test), and answered — not just mentioned · □ ≥ 250 words.
- **Formative:** in the steelman drill, does the child's version of the other side sound like something a real opponent would say, or like a punching bag?
- **Summative (per `obj.writing.68.compose.02`):** checklist passes on 2 pieces.
- **If not yet:** counterargument missing or strawmanned → run the drill orally with roles swapped: *child* argues the side they disagree with for two minutes, parent scores fairness. Claim unarguable ("dogs exist") → sort a parent-made list into arguable / not-arguable before the next attempt.

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** writing app, reader, parent review/sign-off.
- **Blocked on new tool:** nothing.

## 6. Authoring notes

- Anchor: current 6–8 anchor (INTEGRATION.md §1). Book-rooted claims are the default (Silver's villainy; whether Buck's return to the wild is a happy ending; whether Tom Sawyer is a good friend) — evidence then means *lines*, not vibes. The bank also carries research-rooted persuasion (Kiwix pairings) for variety.
- Prereq: `lesson.writing.68.compose.01` — multi-paragraph structure is assumed here, not taught.
- Prompt bank: [`../prompt-banks/68.md`](../prompt-banks/68.md).
- Misconception to watch: "the counterargument weakens my essay." It's the opposite and the lesson says so out loud — an answered objection is the strongest paragraph in the piece.
