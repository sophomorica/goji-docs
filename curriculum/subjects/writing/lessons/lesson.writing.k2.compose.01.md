## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can write a three-sentence recount of something that happened — a fable just read or a real moment — with the sentences in time order: first, then, last.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 2 | Parent tells today's breakfast **out of order**: "Last I ate the toast. First I woke up. Then I made toast." Child giggles and fixes it — order is the whole trick, and they already know it | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 5 | **Parent models live** (animation: none — modeled live): parent writes their own 3-sentence recount of the fable just read, thinking aloud: "First… what happened first? *The crow found a piece of cheese.*" One sentence per finger: first, then, last. Child watches sentences get capitals and end marks as they land | (off-device, `aesop-fables`) | none — modeled live |
| 3 | **Practice** | 8 | Child says their recount **aloud first** — three fingers, three sentences (oral rehearsal is the K–2 prewrite). Parent echoes it back; only then does the child type it in the journal. Parent alongside for the first one | journal | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 6 | Child writes a second recount solo — a different fable or "my morning" — from the prompt bank. Parent reviews against the checklist below and signs off | journal | — |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Reading — one fable from `aesop-fables` (aloud is fine)
- [ ] Task 2: Journal — "Recount the fable you just read in 3 sentences: what happened first, then, and last." (starter ideas: [`../prompt-banks/k2.md`](../prompt-banks/k2.md))
  - Checklist: 3 complete sentences — each has a naming part and a telling part · In time order: first, then, last · All three sentences about the same story · At least 25 words
- [ ] Task 3: Parent review — checklist sign-off (writing is parent-judged, never auto-scored)

## 4. Checks

- **Parent checklist (the gate):** □ 3 complete sentences (naming + telling part) · □ in time order — swapping any two would break the story · □ on topic — all three about the same event. Word floor ≥ 25 (journal counts it).
- **Formative:** during oral rehearsal, does the child's "then" sentence actually follow from the "first" sentence, or is it a new topic?
- **Summative (per `obj.writing.k2.compose.01`):** checklist passes on 2 different recounts, different days.
- **If not yet:** order failing → retell the fable with three picture cards or objects physically arranged left-to-right, then write from the row. Fragments creeping in → drop back to `lesson.writing.k2.grammar.01` repair game before the next recount.

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** journal (word counts sync), reader, parent review/sign-off.
- **Blocked on new tool:** nothing. (No animation by design this pass — the teach step is the parent modeling real writing, which is the thing itself.)

## 6. Authoring notes

- Anchor: `aesop-fables` — fables are short enough that beginning/middle/end fits three sentences exactly; that's why they lead.
- Prompt bank: [`../prompt-banks/k2.md`](../prompt-banks/k2.md) — recount entries carry the same checklist as rubric lines (WRITING_LOOP_SPEC §2b shape, manual mode today).
- Misconception to watch: the "and then and then and then" chain — one breathless sentence instead of three. The three-finger ritual is the antidote: each finger gets its own capital and end mark.
