## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the learner keeps subject-verb agreement, pronoun reference, and verb tense consistent across long sentences and whole paragraphs — the errors that only appear once sentences get ambitious.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 3 | Parent reads: "When Anne met Diana at the brook, she was nervous." Who was nervous? The learner realizes they *cannot know* — the sentence is grammatical and still broken. That's the theme: long sentences fail quietly | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 5 | Three failure modes with one fix-question each: (a) agreement at distance — "the box of old letters **was**": find the real subject, ignore the nouns crowding the verb; (b) pronoun reference — every he/she/it/they must point at exactly one thing; if two candidates, rename or restructure; (c) tense drift — pick the timeline and hold it for the paragraph | (off-device) | — |
| 3 | **Practice** | 10 | Editing pass on two prepared paragraph-length passages (book-flavored, errors injected — see authoring notes): learner finds and fixes, then *names which failure mode* each error was. Naming the mode is the skill — it's what makes their own revision systematic | writing | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 8 | Quiz: agreement-at-distance / ambiguous-pronoun / tense-drift items; then apply: revise one of the learner's own recent journal or writing-app paragraphs for all three modes | parent quiz, writing, journal | `quiz.writing.68.grammar.02` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Parent quiz — `quiz.writing.68.grammar.02`, pass ≥ 8/10
- [ ] Task 2: Writing (via journal) — "Take one paragraph you wrote recently and do a self-edit pass: fix the errors, and mark each fix A (agreement), R (reference), or T (tense)."
  - Checklist: One of your own paragraphs, re-read slowly · Every fix marked A, R, or T · Find the real subject of each long sentence — does the verb agree? · Read it aloud — would a listener ask 'who's she?'
- [ ] Task 3: Reading — 20 min; find one long sentence that stays perfectly clear and say why it works

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** during the editing pass, does the learner find the **real subject** of a long sentence on request? That single move underwrites the whole agreement half.
- **Summative:** quiz ≥ 8/10 on paragraph-length items (per `obj.writing.68.grammar.02`) + a marked-up self-edit.
- **If not yet:** shrink the radius — practice agreement on two-noun sentences ("The box of letters was…") until automatic, then re-lengthen. For reference errors, the read-aloud-to-someone test: if the listener asks "who's *she*?", the sentence failed; ears catch ambiguity that eyes forgive.

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** parent quiz, writing app, journal, reader.
- **Blocked on new tool:** nothing hard-blocked. Passage-style quiz items (a paragraph with numbered underlined spots) may strain the current parent-quiz item format — if so, deliver each spot as its own item; flag in TOOLS.md only if that workaround fails in practice.

## 6. Authoring notes

- Prereq: `lesson.writing.68.grammar.01` (clause sight — you can't track agreement across clauses you can't see).
- Quiz blueprint: `quiz.writing.68.grammar.02` in [`../quiz-blueprints.md`](../quiz-blueprints.md).
- Practice passages: adapt from on-device books (`anne-green-gables`, `call-of-wild` — ids in [`../../reading/reading-lists.md`](../../reading/reading-lists.md)) with errors injected. Keep the originals on hand: comparing the fixed version against the author's actual sentence is the best possible feedback.
- Misconception to watch: proximity agreement — matching the verb to the nearest noun instead of the subject. It's the single most common item-miss in this strand; the quiz weights it accordingly.
