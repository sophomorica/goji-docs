## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can make a claim about *Black Beauty* — how a character feels, why something happened — and prove it with details from the actual text, at least one quoted or closely retold.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 3 | Parent claims something *wrong* on purpose: "Ginger trusts people completely." Child protests — "No! She bites because—" Parent: "Prove it. Show me the line." The whole lesson is that move | (off-device, `black-beauty`) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 6 | **Parent models live** (animation: none — modeled live): write a claim-and-proof response aloud. Claim: "Beauty is afraid during the fire, but he obeys anyway." Then hunt the reader for the proof lines and weave one in: *the book says…* Show the difference between **proof from the book** ("James led him out blindfolded") and **proof from my head** ("horses hate fire") | reader | none — modeled live |
| 3 | **Practice** | 8 | Guided: parent gives a claim about the chapter just read; child finds 2 proof details in the reader (word-lookups and re-reading encouraged), marks which one they'll quote, and says how each proves the claim. Then writes it | reader, writing | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 8 | Solo response from the prompt bank — child picks or is assigned a Black Beauty prompt, writes ≥ 100 words with claim + 2 proofs (1 from the lines). Parent reviews against checklist | writing, reader | — |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Reading — `black-beauty` chapter + minutes
- [ ] Task 2: Writing (via journal) — "From the Black Beauty chapter you just read: write an evidence response — make a claim about the book and prove it with lines from the text." (starter ideas: [`../prompt-banks/35.md`](../prompt-banks/35.md))
  - Checklist: A claim about the book (not a summary, not 'I liked it') · At least 2 evidence details — one quoted or closely retold from the actual lines · Say HOW the evidence proves your claim · At least 100 words
- [ ] Task 3: Parent review — checklist sign-off; a strong response also counts toward `obj.reading.35.comp.02` (inference with evidence)

## 4. Checks

- **Parent checklist (the gate):** □ claim is about the book (not a summary, not "I liked it") · □ ≥ 2 evidence details, at least 1 quoted or closely retold from the actual lines · □ says **how** the evidence proves the claim, not just lists it · □ ≥ 100 words.
- **Formative:** during the hunt, does the child point at *lines* or at memories? Book-open responding is the habit being built.
- **Summative (per `obj.writing.35.compose.02`):** checklist passes on 2 responses.
- **If not yet:** evidence-from-my-head persisting → play "book or brain?": parent reads the child's own details back and the child sorts each one. Claim missing (pure retell) → give the claim as a sentence starter ("Beauty's hardest day is ___ because…") until claims come unprompted.

## 5. Tools

- **Uses existing:** reader (`black-beauty` is on device), writing app, parent review/sign-off.
- **Blocked on new tool:** nothing.

## 6. Authoring notes

- Anchor: `black-beauty` — **direct reading tie-in by design**: it's the 3–5 rotation's inference book (first-person clues, INTEGRATION.md §1), so claim-and-proof writing and `obj.reading.35.comp.02` are the same muscle from two sides. One submission can credit both pillars (WRITING_LOOP_SPEC §4).
- Prereq: `lesson.writing.35.compose.01` — the response *is* a paragraph; the machine holds it up.
- Research crossover when a prompt wants context: Kiwix topics for this anchor are horses, the Victorian era, cabs and carriages, animal-welfare history (INTEGRATION.md §4).
- Misconception to watch: quoting as decoration — a line dropped in that proves nothing. The checklist's "says how" item exists for exactly this.
