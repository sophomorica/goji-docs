## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the learner can tell a clause (has its own subject + verb) from a phrase (doesn't), tell independent from dependent, and deliberately build complex sentences with subordinating conjunctions and relative clauses.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook | 3 | From `treasure-island`, a genuinely long sentence (Stevenson supplies plenty). Challenge: how many *complete little sentences* are hiding inside this one? Count subject-verb pairs together | reader | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 5 | Watch: the clause test (does it have its own subject + verb?), independent vs dependent (the because-word makes a clause lean), phrases as fuel with no engine | animation (parent device preview for now) | `anim.writing.68.grammar.01` |
| 3 | **Practice** | 10 | Two rounds: (a) dissection — 5 sentences from the current book; underline each clause, box each phrase, label clauses I or D; (b) construction — combine given sentence pairs into complex sentences three ways (leading dependent clause + comma; trailing dependent clause; relative clause with who/which/that) | reader, (off-device) | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 8 | Quiz: clause-or-phrase / spot the fragment / combine correctly; then journal: one paragraph on today's reading containing at least 2 deliberate complex sentences, labeled | parent quiz, journal, writing | `quiz.writing.68.grammar.01` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Parent quiz — `quiz.writing.68.grammar.01`, pass ≥ 8/10
- [ ] Task 2: Journal — reading-response paragraph with 2 labeled complex sentences
- [ ] Task 3: Reading — 20 min; flag one sentence with 3+ clauses and bring it to dinner

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** in dissection, the tell is participial phrases ("running down the beach") — they *feel* clause-like because a verb-ish word is present. Apply the test out loud: who is the subject *inside* it? None → phrase.
- **Summative:** quiz ≥ 8/10 + journal shows deliberate complex sentences (per `obj.writing.68.grammar.01`).
- **If not yet:** drop back to the two-question test on every word group — (1) subject? (2) its own verb? — until it's mechanical, using only short made examples; book sentences return once the test is automatic. The because-fragment ("Because the map was torn.") gets its own mini-round: it *has* subject and verb, and still can't stand alone.
