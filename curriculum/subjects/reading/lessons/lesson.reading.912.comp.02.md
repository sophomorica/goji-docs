## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson (run as a routine across one full novel), the child can trace a theme, a recurring symbol, and one character's development from first page to last — and argue the connection in essay-form prose.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook (discussion, session 1 only) | 5 | Choose the novel and set the three tracking threads together: one **theme candidate**, one **symbol**, one **character**. *Frankenstein*: what knowledge costs / light & fire / Victor. *Pride and Prejudice*: how pride blinds judgment / first impressions & letters / Elizabeth. Threads are provisional — revising them mid-book is doing it right | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** (session 1 only) | 5 | Set up a three-thread tracking page in notebooks (Theme / Symbol / Character), one dated entry per session: quote or moment + chapter + one line on what it adds to the thread. This is the 6–8 running summary grown up — entries argue, not just record | notebooks | — |
| 3 | **Practice** (every session) | 25 | Read the session's chapter-chunk; add at least one entry to any thread (all three by every second session). Symbol entries must note what the symbol is *doing this time* — a symbol that always means the same thing isn't being read closely | reader (`pride-prejudice` or `frankenstein` — needs ingest), notebooks | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** (mid-book + book end) | 10 | Mid-book: one-paragraph journal essay — "Is your theme candidate surviving contact with the book? Revise or defend." Book end: full essay-form response in journal, thesis + evidence from the tracking page, connecting all three threads ("How does [symbol] mark [character]'s change, and what does that argue?"). Parent reviews against the rubric below | journal, notebooks | — |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Reading — `frankenstein` (or `pride-prejudice`), 25 min *(blocked until ingest)*
- [ ] Task 2: Notebooks — thread entries (theme/symbol/character), dated, with chapter refs
- [ ] Task 3: Journal — "Mid-book and at the book's end: write an essay-form response to your novel, built from your notebook threads (theme, symbol, character)."
  - Checklist: An arguable thesis about the book · At least 3 pieces of tracked evidence with chapter refs · All three threads — theme, symbol, character — connected · A conclusion that says something the intro couldn't · Parent reviews when done

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** thread entries each session — quote-only entries (no "what it adds" line) mean collecting, not reading; symbol entries that repeat the same meaning mean the thread has gone dead. Both get one nudge, not a redo.
- **Summative:** parent review of the final essay (per OBJECTIVES.md: essay-form responses, parent review — essay rubric TBD; until it lands, review against: arguable thesis, ≥ 3 pieces of tracked evidence with chapter refs, all three threads connected, conclusion says something the intro couldn't).
- **If not yet:** essay retells plot → rebuild from the tracking page only, book closed: thesis first, then pick the 3 entries that prove it. Threads too thin to write from → don't fake it; reopen the book for a targeted hunt on the weakest thread (this is legitimate rereading, not failure). Thesis unarguable → run the 6–8 "could someone argue with it?" test from `lesson.reading.68.comp.01` — same muscle, harder book.

## 6. Authoring notes

- Prereq: `obj.reading.912.comp.01` (close reading) — thread entries assume the child can already say what a line is *doing*.
- This is a **routine lesson**: teach steps once, practice repeats ~12–15 sessions for either novel. `frankenstein` is the friendlier first pass (shorter, louder symbols); `pride-prejudice` demands the subtler irony ear from `obj.reading.68.comp.02`.
- The notebooks tracking page + journal essays are the writing-pillar crossover — the book-end essay can double as a writing-pillar assignment in the same week rather than duplicating work.
