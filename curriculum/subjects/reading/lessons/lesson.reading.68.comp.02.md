## 1. Goal (one sentence)

After this lesson, the child can explain what a character really wants (vs what they say), whose eyes we see through, and spot irony — starting with Tom Sawyer getting paid to skip his own chore.

## 2. Sequence (teach → practice → comprehension)

| Step | Pattern | Minutes | Activity | Goji tool | Media |
|------|---------|---------|----------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Hook (discussion) | 3 | "Tom is forced to whitewash a fence. By the end, boys are *paying him* for the privilege. How?" Let them retell it; steer toward "what did Tom *say* vs what did Tom *want*?" | (off-device) | — |
| 2 | **Teach** | 5 | Parent-led mini-lesson (no animation): three questions to carry into any scene — 1) What does the character want? 2) Who is telling us, and what can't they see? 3) Is the author winking — does the surface say one thing and mean another? Write the three on paper as a bookmark | (off-device) | — |
| 3 | **Practice** | 17 | Reread the fence scene (Ch. 2) in the reader with the bookmark questions; then journal analysis: Tom's stated reason vs real motive, one line that proves the real motive, and where the narrator winks at us | reader (`tom-sawyer`), journal | — |
| 4 | **Comprehension** | 8 | Quiz: motive inference, point-of-view identification, irony spotting — every inference item pairs with "which line tells you?" | parent quiz | `quiz.reading.68.comp.02` |

## 3. School Day mapping (how a parent would assign today)

- [ ] Task 1: Reading — `tom-sawyer`, 20 min (include the fence scene, Ch. 2)
- [ ] Task 2: Journal — "Think about what you just read in Tom Sawyer. Analyze one character's motive in four parts: stated reason, real want, proving line, and where the author winks."
  - Checklist: The character's stated reason (what they say) · Their real want (what they actually want) · A proving line from the book · Where the author winks — how you know more than the character admits
- [ ] Task 3: Parent quiz — `quiz.reading.68.comp.02`, pass ≥ 4/5

## 4. Checks

- **Formative:** during the hook retell — does the child notice Tom is *performing* enjoyment, or do they take his words at face value? Face-value reading is the tell that step 2 needs to go slower.
- **Summative:** quiz ≥ 4/5 (with evidence lines on inference items) + one journal analysis passing parent review.
- **If not yet:** motive misses → shrink to one question ("what does Tom want?") on a single paragraph before re-widening. Irony misses → that's normal at this band; give a second worked example from `treasure-island` (Long John Silver's friendliness) and ask only "does he mean it?" — don't gate the objective on irony alone until motive and POV are solid.
