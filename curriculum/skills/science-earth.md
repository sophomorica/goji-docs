## Summary

**Graduation role (GRADUATION.md):** Earth Science counts toward the required **3 laboratory-science credits across two disciplines**, and it is the pragmatic second discipline for a home setting: ES.1's practices lean on **map, chart, and data interpretation** — screen-friendly work — far more than wet-lab bench skills. The honest constraint still stands (field observation, mineral testing = off-device, parent-led with a rock/mineral kit), but proportionally less of this course is out of reach than Biology, Chemistry, or Physics.

1. **Sequence ES as the graduation-strategy lab science** — pair with Biology to satisfy the two-discipline rule with the least off-device dependency; say so in the parent-facing course plan.
2. **Plate tectonics + geologic time spine** (ES.5/ES.7/ES.9) — one continuous unit from the G5 "Restless Earth" intro through dating methods; ordering/superposition items are cheap, diagnostic quizzes.
3. **Virginia geology + Chesapeake capstone** (ES.7/ES.8/ES.10) — Appalachians, karst caves (tom-sawyer hook), and the Bay unify the local thread running since grade 4; ends in an evidence-based policy essay.
