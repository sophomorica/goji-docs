# Economics and Personal Finance (EPF) — 2019 Virginia SOLs × Goji coverage

Verified source: VDOE *Economics and Personal Finance Standards of Learning* (doe.virginia.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/2004/…, adopted October 17, 2019, fetched 2026-08-07 via archive). 18 standards. **EPF is its own 1-credit graduation requirement** — separate from the History & Social Sciences ×3 credits (GRADUATION.md); typically taken in high school, no verified credit attached. Delivery stance (PRACTICES.md §6): Kiwix-anchored units + PDF lessons + quizzes via the research→notebooks chain (INTEGRATION.md §2/§4); the personal-finance half is unusually workbook-shaped — real budgets, real forms — so PDF lessons and notebooks projects carry more weight than elsewhere.

## Summary

EPF is the **separate 1-credit graduation requirement** and the catalog's most practical course: half economics (EPF.1–9, which spirals CE.11–14 and GOVT.13–14 — build shared units, credit all three courses), half life skills (EPF.10–18, which want real forms, real budgets, and real numbers more than articles). The Goji angle: notebooks can host a genuinely *live* budget and cost-comparison projects, and the math crossovers (amortization, compound interest) let one lesson pay two catalogs. Highest-value next steps:

1. **Personal budget capstone** (EPF.16) — a running notebooks budget with monthly parent review; the single most valuable life-skill artifact the device can hold.
2. **Compound interest + amortization pair** (EPF.13/EPF.17) — author once with the Algebra II exponential-functions lane; quiz gates live in both catalogs.
3. **Forms literacy PDF pack** (EPF.12/EPF.15/EPF.18) — checking-account forms, W-4/W-2/1040, FAFSA/award letter — printable, parent-checked, journal-recorded.
