## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: mood isn't magic — it's built from three visible choices (word choice, sound, pacing), and a close reader can point to each one doing its work in the line.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–12s | Goji seal beside two versions of the same report. Plain card: "It was a quiet, cloudy autumn day." Below it, Poe's line fades in: "…a dull, dark, and soundless day in the autumn of the year…" The plain card stays flat; the Poe card dims the scene around it | "Same facts. Only one of them makes you feel something. That feeling has a blueprint" |
| 12–35s | **Tool 1 — word choice.** In the Poe line, *dull*, *dark*, *soundless* each underline in turn; ghost-swaps flicker above them (*quiet* → *soundless*) and the felt weight visibly drops on the swap, returns on the original | "Every word was chosen over a plainer twin. *Soundless* isn't *quiet* — quiet is peaceful; soundless is wrong. Word choice is the first tool" |
| 35–55s | **Tool 2 — sound.** The same three words pulse on their initial consonants — *d…d…s* — a soft waveform ripples under the line as it's read slowly aloud | "Read it aloud: dull, dark… the sounds repeat like a slow bell. Sound works on you even when you read silently" |
| 55–78s | **Tool 3 — pacing.** New line, from "The Tell-Tale Heart": "TRUE! —nervous —very, very dreadfully nervous I had been and am…" The dashes flash as hard stops; the fragments stack jaggedly against the earlier long, even Usher sentence shown for contrast | "And pacing: dashes, stumbles, a word said twice — the sentence *breathes* like the narrator. Long and even calms; broken and repeating panics" |
| Close | The three tools dock as small labeled marks (W / S / P) beside an annotation margin; the seal hands off a highlighter-style cursor. Calm end card | "Mood is what the text makes *you* feel. Tone is the narrator's own attitude. Now open the tale — and catch the tools working" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the Goji seal as guide; the plain-vs-Poe contrast (same facts, different build); each tool caught in the act on a **real public-domain Poe line** (Usher opening; Tell-Tale Heart opening); the ghost-swap move (word vs plainer twin) — it's the exact move the notebook annotations ask for; the W/S/P marks (they match the lesson's annotation key); one clean mood-vs-tone sentence at the close.
- **Avoid:** horror styling — no jump cuts, no shock stingers, no gore imagery; the brand stays calm even when the text isn't (dimming and stillness carry the dread). No "mood = what happens in the plot" framing; no implying Poe is the only mood-builder; no more than one line on screen at a time; no confetti.

## What happens next on Goji

Practice: close-read "The Tell-Tale Heart" in the reader (post-ingest), annotate in notebooks with the W/S/P key (≥ 6 annotations), then `quiz.reading.912.comp.01`.
