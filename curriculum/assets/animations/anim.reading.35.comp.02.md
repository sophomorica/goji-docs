## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: authors leave clues instead of labels; a clue from the text plus something you already know makes an inference — and you must be able to point at the clue.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–12s | A Black Beauty line appears: *"my ears went back, and I began to tremble."* Goji seal, magnifying glass out: the book never SAYS a feeling | "The book doesn't say 'Beauty was afraid.' So how do we know he was?" |
| 12–35s | The inference machine: slot 1 CLUE (ears back, trembling) + slot 2 WHAT YOU KNOW (animals tremble when scared) → gears turn → out slides AFRAID | "Clue from the book + what you know = what the author didn't say" |
| 35–55s | The prove-it rule: the AFRAID card only stays on the board when the quoted line is pinned under it. An unpinned guess ("Beauty was hungry?") slides right off | "No clue line, no claim. Inferences you can't point to are just guesses" |
| Close | Magnifying glass handed to the viewer | "The clues are waiting. Go find them" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** a real (or lightly adapted) anchor-book line; the two-slot machine (clue + knowledge, both required); the pin-the-quote rule — it's the same evidence rule the quiz enforces.
- **Avoid:** implying inference = wild guessing; clue-free "reading minds"; more than one worked example (one deep beats three shallow).

## What happens next on Goji

Practice tool: reader (Black Beauty chunk) → claim + proof-line answers with parent → chapter quiz (evidence lines stored) → journal exit ticket.

## Brand / motion notes

Calm, clear, skippable; Pi-friendly; seal-red/cream; Goji seal guide. No confetti. Respect reduced-motion.
