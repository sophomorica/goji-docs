## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: some solids dissolve in water and some never do; **dissolved is not destroyed** — the material is still there in pieces too small to see (taste it, or evaporate the water and it returns); liquids can refuse to mix (oil floats in its own layer); and **hot water dissolves things faster** than cold.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–20s | Beaker; salt grains swirl and fade; a fine even sparkle spreads through the whole water | "Salt vanishes from sight — but it's STILL THERE, in pieces too small to see. Taste it. Or dry the water — the salt comes back." |
| 20–35s | Beaker; sand grains sink through the water and pile on the bottom | "Sand NEVER dissolves. Stir all you like — it settles." |
| 35–50s | Beaker; golden oil layer floating sharply on top of the water | "Oil refuses to mix — it floats in its own layer." |
| 50–70s | Two beakers, HOT (steam) vs COLD (ice cube); sugar cube shrinks fast in hot, barely in cold | "The race: HOT wins. Hot water bits jiggle faster and pull the sugar apart sooner." |
| Close (70–90s) | Three rule emblems | "Dissolved is NOT destroyed · not everything dissolves · heat speeds it up." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the still-there proof in the same beat as the vanishing (sparkle spread + taste/evaporate line) — never leave "it disappeared" as the last word; sand settling despite stirring; the hot/cold race side-by-side with the SAME size sugar cubes; the sharp oil/water boundary.
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** never show dissolved material simply ceasing to exist ("gone forever"); never show sand dissolving under vigorous stirring ("stir hard enough"); never show hot and cold behaving alike; no faces or eyes on beakers, spoons, or materials; no bubbling mad-science glassware; calm single-beaker scenes.

## What happens next on Goji
