## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: some letter pairs (sh, ch, th) melt into ONE brand-new sound that neither letter makes alone — while blends (st, gr, fl) keep both sounds, just glued fast.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–10s | Goji seal tries to read S-H-I-P one letter at a time: /s/…/h/… — the tiles rattle, it doesn't work. The `s` and `h` look at each other | "Some letters can't do it alone…" |
| 10–30s | The `s` and `h` tiles MELT together into one glowing tile: "shhh" (finger-to-lips seal). SH-I-P slides: "ship!" A ship sails on. Same melt: C-H → "ch" (a little train chuff), CH-A-T: "chat!" | "S and h melt into a brand-new sound: shhh! Ship! C and h? Ch! Chat!" |
| 30–50s | Contrast: S-T-A-G — the `s` and `t` do NOT melt; they stay two tiles holding hands and slide extra fast: "s-t… stag!" (the fable's stag leaps past). One more: G-R-A-SS, "grass!" | "But s and t don't melt — they're a blend. Both sounds, glued fast! Stag! Grass!" |
| Close | Two piles: melted single tiles (sh, ch, th) vs holding-hands pairs (st, gr, fl); the melted ones glow | "Melted team = one new sound. Holding hands = both sounds, fast!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the melt (digraph = one new sound) visually different from the hand-hold (blend = both sounds kept); a failed letter-by-letter attempt BEFORE the melt, so the child feels why the team exists; anchor-book words (stag, grass — the ship/chat pair carries the digraphs) with the blend/digraph distinction stated in kid terms.
- **Avoid:** the words "digraph" and "blend" as the anchor language (melted team / holding hands is the anchor; grown-up terms okay in small on-screen text once); treating th's two voices (thin vs that) — one "th" is enough for this clip; blends with more than two letters (str, spl) — not this clip; more than 2 example words per idea.

## What happens next on Goji

Practice tool: flashcards decks `read-k2-digraphs` then `read-k2-blends` (anchor-sampled, minimal pairs ship/sip, stop/top), read aloud to parent; then typing the same words.
