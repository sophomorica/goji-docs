## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: when two vowels stand together, the first one says its name and the second stays silent — and when `r` stands behind a vowel, it changes the vowel's voice into one new chunk.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–10s | Goji seal reads M-E-T: "met!" Then a second vowel tile `a` walks in beside the `e` — the two vowels link arms | "Two vowels, side by side. But teams have ONE captain…" |
| 10–30s | The first vowel (`e`) lights up and says its NAME: "E!" — the `a` zips its lips. Tiles slide: "meat!" A slab of meat appears (the dog-and-bone fable's prize). Same move on R-A-I-N: `a` speaks, `i` silent — "rain!" | "The first vowel says its name. The second one keeps quiet. Meat! Rain!" |
| 30–50s | New scene: an `r` marches up behind an `a`. Instead of a name, the pair fuses into one growling chunk: "ar!" — C-AR-T: "cart!" Then "or": C-OR-N, "corn!" (the crow's stolen corn) | "But watch out for bossy r — it grabs the vowel and changes its voice. Ar! Or!" |
| Close | A row of team words from the fables: sheep, goat, road, farm; the first vowel of each team glints | "Spot the team — first vowel talks. Spot the r — new voice!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the two vowels visibly *together as a unit* before any sound; first-vowel-speaks-second-silent shown identically for ai/ee/oa/ea; bossy-r as a *different* move (fusion into one new sound, not a name); anchor-book words (meat, rain, corn, sheep, goat) so the animation's words appear in tonight's fable.
- **Avoid:** the old rhyme "when two vowels go walking…" stated as an always-rule (it fails often — show these four teams only, claim nothing universal); sounding the team letter-by-letter even once; exceptions (ea in "bread", "great") — not this clip; more than 2 example words per pattern.

## What happens next on Goji

Practice tool: flashcards decks `read-k2-vteams` then `read-k2-bossy-r` (anchor-sampled), read aloud to parent; then typing the same words.
