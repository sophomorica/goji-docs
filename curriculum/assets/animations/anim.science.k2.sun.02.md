## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: the sun sits low where it rises in the morning, high overhead at noon, and low where it sets in the evening; a shadow is longest when the sun is low and shortest at noon; the sun warms land, air and water; at night the sun still shines — on the other side of the turning Earth.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–15s | One tree; plain sun low left; long shadow stretching right | "MORNING. The sun is LOW, where it rises. The shadow is long." |
| 15–30s | Sun glides high overhead; shadow shrinks to a stub | "NOON — lunchtime. The sun is HIGH. The shadow is at its shortest." |
| 30–45s | Sun glides low right; shadow stretches long left | "EVENING. The sun sinks LOW where it sets. Long shadow again." |
| 45–60s | Sun high; warm shimmer on soil, wavy air lines, glinting pond | "All day the sun WARMS: the land, the air, the water." |
| 60–80s | Sky darkens to stars, faint glow beyond the horizon; then dawn returns, sun low left | "At night the sun isn't gone — it never turns off. The Earth turns, so it shines on the other side. It's not alive, and it doesn't follow you. Morning: there it is again." |
| Close | Dotted arc with three suns: low–high–low | "Low, high, low — the sun's day trip, every day." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** one continuous scene (same tree) so the shadow's swing and shrink is visibly caused by the sun's position; the shadow always on the side away from the sun; the warmer beat touching all three of land, air, water; the night beat resolving back to morning.
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** **no face, eyes, or smile on the sun** (feeds sun-alive); never show the sun "going to sleep", diving into the sea, or switching off (sun-disappears); shadow never on the sun's side; no compass letters (say "where it rises / where it sets"); night not scary; no confetti.
