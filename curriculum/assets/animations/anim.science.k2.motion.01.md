## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: things move in four nameable ways — **straight**, **circular** (travels around a path), **spinning** (turns in one spot — NOT the same as circular), and **back-and-forth** — and that **sound is made by vibration**: something moving back and forth very fast, like a plucked rubber band.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–15s | Museum stand 1: an arrow-marked ball slides left to right along a dashed straight line | "Some things move STRAIGHT. One line — there it goes!" |
| 15–30s | Stand 2: a merry-go-round horse travels around a visible dashed circle path | "Some things move in a CIRCLE. The horse travels around a path, around and around." |
| 30–46s | Stand 3: a top spins fast in ONE spot on a marked X; the horse's circle path stays ghosted beside it for contrast | "Some things SPIN. The top turns in ONE spot. It does not travel around a path like the horse. Spinning and circular are different!" |
| 46–60s | Stand 4: a swing goes forward and back on a dashed arc, again and again | "Some things move BACK AND FORTH. Forward… back… forward… back." |
| Close (60–85s) | Stand 5: a stretched rubber band is plucked; it blurs back-and-forth very fast; sound waves ripple out FROM the band to an ear icon; stamp VIBRATES → SOUND | "Pluck! The rubber band moves back and forth very fast. That is VIBRATING. Vibrating makes the sound you HEAR. Every sound comes from something moving!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the top staying pinned to one marked spot while the horse visibly travels a drawn circular path (the contrast is the lesson); the swing repeating (not one trip); sound waves starting AT the vibrating band, only while it vibrates, stopping when it stops; the back-and-forth badge lighting again on the vibration beat ("vibrating IS fast back-and-forth").
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** never one visual that could read as both spinning and circular (no spinning object drawn on a circular path); no sound waves appearing without a moving source ("sound just happens"); no faces or eyes on the top, ball, sled, drum, or any object; ≤ 2 exhibits on screen at once; no confetti; plain cream museum background.

## What happens next on Goji

Practice: in-package watch-and-tap museum — 9 rounds (ball, top, merry-go-round horse, swing, drum skin, sled, guitar string, coin, ferris-wheel car), wrong taps name the exact mix-up (spinning-vs-circular; sound-just-happens). Then the 5-item check, then the rubber-band-and-spoon sound hunt with the parent → journal sentence.
