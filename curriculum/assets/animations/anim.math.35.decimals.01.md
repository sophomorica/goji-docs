## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: place value keeps going to the right of the ones — the decimal point just marks where the ones live; a dime is 1/10 of a dollar = 0.1, a penny is 1/100 = 0.01, and 0.5 is ten times 0.05, no matter how the digits look.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–12s | Goji seal holds up one dollar (a clean rectangle labeled **1**). It splits into 10 equal dime-strips; one strip glows | "One dollar. Cut it into ten equal pieces — each piece is a dime. One dime is one *tenth* of the dollar: 0.1" |
| 12–28s | The glowing dime-strip splits into 10 tiny penny-squares; one glows | "Cut a dime into ten — each tiny piece is a penny. One *hundredth* of the dollar: 0.01" |
| 28–45s | The familiar place-value chart (hundreds–tens–ones) slides in, then *grows rightward*: a dot appears after the ones, then tenths and hundredths columns. Coins fall into their columns | "You know this chart. It doesn't stop at the ones! The dot is just a marker: ones live here. Tenths next door. Hundredths after that" |
| 45–60s | Build **$1.34**: one dollar into the ones column, 3 dimes into tenths, 4 pennies into hundredths; the numeral 1.34 assembles digit by digit above the columns | "One dollar, three dimes, four pennies — one and thirty-four hundredths: 1.34" |
| 60–72s | Trap scene: **0.5** vs **0.05** side by side as coins — 5 dime-strips vs 5 penny-squares, sizes glaringly different | "Five dimes… or five pennies? 0.5 is ten times as much as 0.05. Never judge a decimal by how long it looks — check *which column* the digits live in" |
| Close | Goji seal stacks the coins back into one whole dollar; end card | "The chart keeps going. Go price something!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** money as the concrete anchor (dollar → dimes → pennies, physically cut); the place-value chart *extending* rightward (decimals as more place value, not a new topic); the decimal point introduced as the ones-marker, not "the start of the decimal"; the 0.5-vs-0.05 contrast with visible size difference.
- **Avoid:** thousandths in the animation (keep to money precision — thousandths arrive in the lesson/quiz by extension); reading decimals digit-string style ("point twenty-five") — always place-value names ("twenty-five hundredths"); anything that rewards longer-is-bigger (never show 0.125 "beating" 0.5); fraction notation beyond 1/10 and 1/100 (this is the decimals reveal, the fraction bridge lives in the lesson); confetti or HUD effects.
