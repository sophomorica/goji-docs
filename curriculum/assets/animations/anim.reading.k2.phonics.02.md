## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: a silent-e at the end reaches back over one consonant and makes the vowel say its own name — cap becomes cape.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–10s | Goji seal reads C-A-P: "cap!" A tiny sleeping `a` snores inside the word | "You know this one. But watch what one silent letter can do…" |
| 10–30s | An `e` tiptoes to the end of the word, silent (zipped lips), and taps the `a` awake over the `p`'s head. The `a` sits up and says its NAME: "A!" — "cape!" A cape swirls on | "The e says nothing. It just wakes the vowel to say its name. Cape!" |
| 30–50s | The same move, twice: kit → kite (kite lifts off), hop → hope. The reach-back gesture is identical each time | "Kit… kite! Hop… hope! Silent e, loud vowel" |
| Close | Word pairs side by side; the e glints | "Spot the e at the end — before you say the word!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the e is *silent* (visible zipped lips / no sound wave); the reach-back arc over exactly one consonant; minimal pairs transforming (same tiles, one added).
- **Avoid:** "magic" doing anything except this one job; exceptions (have, love) — not this clip; long-vowel jargon ("long a" okay in text, but "says its name" is the anchor phrase).

## What happens next on Goji

Practice tool: flashcards deck `read-k2-vce` (minimal pairs), then typing both forms of 5 pairs.

## Brand / motion notes

Calm, clear, skippable; Pi-friendly; seal-red/cream; Goji seal guide. No confetti. Respect reduced-motion.
