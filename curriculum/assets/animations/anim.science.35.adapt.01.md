## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: animals survive through **adaptations** — **physical** ones are body parts they were born with (mongoose speed/fur/toughness, tiger camouflage stripes), **behavioral** ones are things they do (wolf pack hunting together, bear's winter sleep); whole **populations** change over many generations without any animal choosing to change; and **fossils** are clues to long-ago animals and environments (fish fossil on a mountain = once under water).

| Close (72–90s) | Mountain summit; split stone reveals a fish fossil; a faint ghost-wash of ancient water shimmers over the peak | "Fossils are clues. A fish up here? This land was once UNDER WATER." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the born-with vs does distinction stated on-screen at both category beats; the generations sequence with at least three steps and chicks (change happens through survival + offspring, not stretching); the fossil read as an inference (fish → water) not a fact stated flat; the mongoose staying on-model (recurring character ref in `assets.json`).
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** never show an animal deciding, wishing, or quickly morphing its body ("adapts on purpose / quickly"); never label an action as a body part or vice versa; never present the fossil as "just an old rock"; no violence — cobra strikes always miss, the wolves never touch the deer; no venom mechanics; no faces on rocks/fossils/stones; no confetti.
