## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: four instruments measure the sky (thermometer °F, barometer = air pressure, anemometer = wind speed, rain gauge); measurements **recorded over days reveal patterns** (falling pressure hints storms) and a forecast is that pattern pointed at tomorrow — evidence, not magic, not a promise; extreme events (thunderstorm, tornado, hurricane, drought) reshape ecosystems and have safety rules (go DOWN to the cellar); weather is one day's sky, climate is the pattern over many years.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–11s | Four tools on the barn board: thermometer, barometer dial, spinning anemometer, rain gauge | "Four tools watch the sky…" (each named with what it measures) |
| 11–20s | Line chart drawing itself: five pressure dots dropping Mon→Fri, red falling line | "Write the readings down every day and patterns appear. Falling pressure hints a storm." |
| 20–27s | "falling pressure + years of records" → arrow → cloud saying "storm LIKELY tomorrow" | "A forecast is evidence at work — not magic, and not a promise." |
| 27–40s | Plain grey funnel far off on the flat prairie; lightning; dry cracked earth; storm-cellar door in the foreground | "Extreme weather reshapes ecosystems… When the siren sounds, go DOWN — into the storm cellar." |
| 40–50s (close) | Split card: TODAY 🌧️ = WEATHER vs stack of MANY YEARS ☀️🌾 = CLIMATE | "Kansas prairie summers are usually hot and dry — that is climate — even if today it rains." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** each tool paired to its measurement; the record as a day-by-day series, not a single reading; "LIKELY" wording on the forecast; the storm-cellar (Dorothy's) as the tornado response; the one-day vs many-years contrast.
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** **a face on the tornado** (plain funnel only); forecast drawn as crystal ball or wizardry (forecasts-are-guesses/magic); "always right" framing (forecast-as-promise); a single cold/hot day labeled climate (weather-equals-climate, cold-week-changes-climate); people shown in danger; faces on sun/clouds/tools.
