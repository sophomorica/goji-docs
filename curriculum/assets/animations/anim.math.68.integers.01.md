## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: the number line continues left of zero; integers are whole-number steps in both directions; absolute value is *distance from zero* (same for 3 and −3); later, addition is a directed move on this same line.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–12s | Familiar number line 0→10; Goji seal walks right to 4 | "You know this line — from zero to the right, numbers get bigger" |
| 12–28s | Line *extends left*; negative ticks appear; seal walks to −3 | "It doesn't stop at zero. Left of zero are the negative integers. −3 is three steps left" |
| 28–45s | Two seals: one at 3, one at −3; both measured with equal-length distance bars to zero; \|3\| and \|−3\| light up as 3 | "Absolute value asks *how far* from zero — not which side. \|−3\| is 3" |
| 45–60s | Order demo: −5, −1, 0, 2 slide into left-to-right order; trap: −8 vs −3, −8 is *less* (farther left) | "Farther left means smaller. −8 is less than −3 — even though 8 looks bigger" |
| 60–72s | Quick add preview (for re-teach use): seal at −2, arrow +5 right → lands on 3 | "Adding positive moves right; adding negative moves left. Same line, always" |
| Close | Seal at zero; end card | "Distance is never negative. Go place some numbers!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** continuous line through zero; absolute value as equal distances; order trap (−8 < −3); integers only (no fractions on the line in this brief).
- **Avoid:** "drop the minus sign" language; implying negatives are not real numbers; thermometer-only metaphor without the abstract line; confetti.
