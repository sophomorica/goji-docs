## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: some forces need a **touch** (a kick is a push), but **gravity** pulls everything **down** with no touch — even light things like feathers (slowly, because air pushes up) — and **magnets** pull or push **some metals** (iron/steel, not all metal, not everything) through air and even through paper; a magnet's two ends behave differently: **different ends snap together, same ends push apart**.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–15s | Boot kicks a red ball; ball rolls away | "Some forces TOUCH. A kick is a push." |
| 15–30s | Apple drops from a branch, straight down arrow to the ground | "GRAVITY pulls with NO touch. It pulls everything DOWN." |
| 30–45s | Rock falls fast; feather falls slowly, swaying, tiny up-arrows for air | "Gravity pulls the feather too — air slows it down." |
| 45–62s | Bar magnet above a sheet of paper; paperclip rises through the air to the paper's underside | "A MAGNET pulls some metals — through air, even paper!" |
| Close (62–85s) | Two magnet pairs: N–N gap widens with outward arrows; N–S pair slides together and snaps | "Two ends: different ends SNAP, same ends PUSH APART." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** a clearly visible air gap in every magnet scene (the pull works at a distance); the feather FALLING (arriving at the ground, just later than the rock); the paper sheet clearly between magnet and clip; N and S labels readable; the crayon/aluminum-can "no pull" cases appear in practice, so the teach must never show the magnet grabbing anything but iron/steel.
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** no faces or eyes on magnets, balls, feathers, cans, apples (non-living stays non-living); never show the feather floating UP or hovering forever (feeds "light things float"); never show a magnet attracting wood/plastic/aluminum (feeds "magnets grab everything/all metal"); no confetti; calm single-idea scenes.
