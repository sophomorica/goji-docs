## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: an **object** and its **material** are different words (a spoon can be wood OR metal); one material makes many objects; **cutting changes the size, never the material** — small pieces of paper are still paper; and light passes through some materials (**all** through glass, **some** through wax paper, **none** through wood) — and **thin does not mean see-through**.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–16s | Two identical-shaped spoons side by side, one wood-grain, one shiny metal; labels OBJECT: spoon → MATERIAL: wood / metal | "Same object — a spoon. Different MATERIALS. One is wood. One is metal. The material is what it is MADE OF." |
| 16–30s | Wood plank at left; a spoon, a chair, and a block slide out of it, all wood-grained | "One material makes many objects. Wood can be a spoon, a chair, a block — all wood!" |
| 30–46s | Scissors cut a patterned sheet of paper into four pieces; each piece keeps the pattern; stamp STILL PAPER | "Snip! Snip! Small pieces now. Are they still paper? YES. Cutting changes the SIZE — never the material." |
| 46–62s | Flashlight beam at window glass: beam passes fully through, bright spot on the far wall; then wax paper: beam dims inside, faint glow behind | "The flashlight test! Glass: ALL the light goes through. Wax paper: only SOME light — a soft glow." |
| Close (62–85s) | Beam at a wood board: stops dead, shadow behind; then a thin ruler: beam still stops; stamp THIN ≠ SEE-THROUGH | "Wood: NO light gets through. Now a thin ruler… still NO light! Thin does not mean see-through." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the two spoons sharing one identical outline (only the fill differs — the object/material split must be visual); every cut piece keeping the paper's pattern; the light beam drawn differently in the three cases — passing (bright far spot), dimming (faint glow), stopping (shadow); the thin ruler blocking light right after the thick board so "thin ≠ see-through" lands as a surprise.
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** no faces or eyes on spoons, chairs, boards, scissors, or any object; never show cut pieces changing color or texture (that would teach cutting-changes-material); never let any light leak past an opaque material; no beam ambiguity on the "some" case; ≤ 3 objects on screen; no confetti; plain cream backgrounds.

## What happens next on Goji

Practice: in-package tester rounds — what-is-it-made-of taps, cut-in-half yes/no (paper, wax), and the flashlight all/some/none sort with a thin-cardboard trap. Then the 5-item check, then the real flashlight test at home with the parent → journal sentence.
