## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: to compare two numbers, compare the tens first — the ones only matter when the tens tie.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–10s | Goji seal referees between 39 and 41. The 9 puffs up proudly next to the 1 | "39 has a NINE. 41 only has a one. So 39 wins… right?" |
| 10–30s | Both numbers unfold into bundles: 39 → 3 tens + 9; 41 → 4 tens + 1. The bundle stacks stand side by side; 4 stacks clearly tower over 3 | "Look at the bundles. Four tens beats three tens — before we even count the loose ones" |
| 30–45s | Tie case: 52 vs 57. Tens stacks match exactly, camera drops to the loose ones; 7 beats 2 | "When the tens tie — then the ones decide" |
| 45–55s | The rule appears as a two-step path: ① tens ② ones. Goji seal stamps it | "Tens first. Ones only for ties." |
| Close | 39 vs 41 replays instantly with the rule; 41 takes a small quiet bow | "41 wins. Now you referee!" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the seductive wrong answer first (big ones digit) and its visual defeat; physical tens stacks side by side; one tie-break case; the two-step rule.
- **Avoid:** the alligator/mouth symbol as the *explanation* (words first; symbol is optional garnish); three-digit numbers; shaming the wrong guess — Goji seal treats it as a great trap, not a dumb mistake.

## What happens next on Goji

Practice tool: math → mental math (comparison set with reversed-digit traps), then `quiz.math.k2.place-value.03`.
