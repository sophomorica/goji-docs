## Learning goal

After watching, the learner understands: a clause is a word group with its own subject + verb; independent clauses stand alone, dependent clauses lean (the subordinating word makes them need support), phrases have no subject-verb engine at all — and combining clauses is how complex sentences are built.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–12s | Goji seal unrolls a long sentence like a banner: "When the storm ended, the crew raised the sails, which hung heavy with rain." Small subject-verb pairs glow inside it, one after another — three little heartbeats | "One sentence. But look — three heartbeats. Three little sentences hiding inside" |
| 12–30s | The banner splits into cards. Card 1: "the crew raised the sails" — stands upright, solid. The clause test runs visibly: subject? check (crew). its own verb? check (raised). | "That's a CLAUSE — its own subject, its own verb. This one stands alone: independent" |
| 30–48s | Card 2: "when the storm ended" — has subject + verb (glows), but the word *when* is drawn as a hook on its edge; the card tilts and leans until it rests against Card 1. Contrast card slides by: "heavy with rain" — no heartbeat glows; it's a flat tile, a phrase | "*When* makes it lean — subject and verb, but it can't stand alone: dependent. And no heartbeat at all? Just a phrase" |
| 48–66s | Building: dependent card + independent card click together two ways — leaning card first (a comma appears at the joint), leaning card last (no comma). Then the trap: the leaning card alone with a period stamped on it… tips over anyway | "Lean it on front — comma at the joint. Lean it on back — no comma. But alone? A period can't hold it up. That's a fragment" |
| Close | The seal taps the finished complex sentence; the three-heartbeat banner reassembles | "Find the heartbeats. Stack the clauses. That's how long sentences hold" |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** the Goji seal as guide; the subject-verb "heartbeat" test applied visibly to every card; the subordinating word drawn as a physical hook/lean; the clause/phrase contrast (phrase = no heartbeat); the comma-when-leading rule as geometry (joint visible); the because/when-fragment trap — has subject and verb, still tips.
- **Avoid:** classifying dependent clauses (adverbial/adjectival/nominal — out of scope); the term "subordinating conjunction" on screen (say/show "the leaning word"; the lesson introduces the term); more than one full combined sentence per beat; run-on repair (that's band 3–5, don't reteach); confetti or HUD effects.
