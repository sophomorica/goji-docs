## Learning goal

After watching, the child understands: when several forces act on one object, the **bigger total** (the net force) decides the motion — equal totals cancel and the object holds still while two forces still act; and the six simple machines are **force-tricks** that trade a **longer path for a smaller push** (lever, ramp/inclined plane, pulley, wheel-and-axle, wedge, screw) — none of them adds free force or does the work for you.

## Beats

| Time | Visual | On-screen text / narration |
|------|--------|----------------------------|
| 0–15s | Tug-of-war, 2 vs 3; force arrows under each team (4 vs 6); rope's red ribbon slides toward the bigger total | "Add each side up: the bigger TOTAL — the net force — wins." |
| 15–30s | Tied tug, 5 vs 5; arrows equal; ribbon dead-still over the chalk line | "A tie: the rope holds still — but TWO forces still act. They cancel." |
| 30–45s | Lever: kid presses long arm down, boulder rises on short end | "The LEVER: a small push on the long arm lifts what hands alone can't." |
| 45–60s | Ramp: barrel rolls up long slope to truck bed; big red 'lift' arrow vs small green 'slope' arrow | "The RAMP: a longer path, a smaller push." |
| 60–75s | Pulley: hand pulls DOWN, flag rides UP; wheel-and-axle beside it (big wheel, small axle) | "The PULLEY turns your pull around. The WHEEL-AND-AXLE: one easy turn does a hard job." |
| Close (75–90s) | Wedge splits log halves apart; screw shown as a ramp spiraling a pole; stamp: "NO FREE FORCE — less push, LONGER path" | "No machine gives free force. You push less — over a longer path. Machines make work easier, never do it for you." |

## Must show / must not show

- **Must:** force arrows labeled with totals so "add each side up" is visible; a tied rope that truly does not move while both arrows stay drawn; the ramp's lift-vs-slope arrow comparison (the distance-for-effort trade); pull-DOWN/flag-UP happening at the same pace; the screw drawn as a wrapped ramp.
- **Avoid (misconceptions, brand, clutter):** never show a machine moving a load with no person applying force (would teach "machines do the work for you / add free force"); never show a moving object with only one arrow when two forces act (guards "if it's moving, only one force acts"); no faces or eyes on rope, rock, barrel, tools; no confetti; calm single-mechanism scenes.
